★★★★★

Demon Copperhead

Barbara Kingsolver · reviewed by Sarah · April 2026

A Pulitzer-winning retelling of David Copperfield set in Appalachian Virginia during the opioid crisis. Kingsolver gives us a narrator who is furious, funny, heartbroken, and utterly alive. The prose never lets you look away, and it earns every tear it wrings out of you.

Our group unanimously loved this one — a rare thing. Best book we've read this year, and possibly in several years.

★★★★

Intermezzo

Sally Rooney · reviewed by Tom · March 2026

Rooney's fourth novel follows two brothers in grief, each in their own complicated relationship. It takes a while to find its footing — the first 80 pages demand patience — but once it does, it's deeply absorbing. She's exploring emotional territory she hasn't touched before.

Split opinions in the group: half loved it, half were frustrated by the slow build. Worth reading either way.

★★★★★

James

Percival Everett · reviewed by Priya · February 2026

A retelling of Huckleberry Finn from the perspective of Jim — now James — that is sharp, devastating, and at times quietly hilarious. Everett uses the form of the 19th-century adventure novel to say something urgent about America, freedom, and the performance of identity.

One of the most discussed books we've ever had. Priya came prepared with three pages of notes. It showed.

★★★

The Women

Kristin Hannah · reviewed by Marcus · January 2026

An ambitious novel following a female Vietnam War nurse across decades. Hannah's storytelling is propulsive, and the subject matter is genuinely underexplored. The pacing stumbles in the second act, but the final third more than redeems it.

The group was divided — some found it deeply moving, others felt it leaned too hard on sentiment. A good one for debate.
