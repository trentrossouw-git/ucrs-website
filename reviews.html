Demon Copperhead

Barbara Kingsolver · reviewed by Sarah · April 2026

FictionPulitzer Winner

A Pulitzer-winning retelling of David Copperfield set in Appalachian Virginia during the opioid crisis. Kingsolver gives us a narrator who is furious, funny, heartbroken, and utterly alive. The prose never lets you look away.

Our group unanimously loved this one — a rare thing. Best book we've read this year, and possibly in several years. Come to the meeting ready for an emotional discussion.

Group verdict: Unanimous favourite
Intermezzo
★★★★

Intermezzo

Sally Rooney · reviewed by Tom · March 2026

FictionLiterary

Rooney's fourth novel follows two brothers in grief, each in their own complicated relationship. It takes a while to find its footing — the first 80 pages demand patience — but once it does, it's deeply absorbing.

Split opinions in the group: half loved it, half were frustrated by the slow build. Worth reading either way. Good one for debate.

Group verdict: Divided — but worth it

James

Percival Everett · reviewed by Priya · February 2026

FictionHistorical

A retelling of Huckleberry Finn from the perspective of Jim — now James — that is sharp, devastating, and at times quietly hilarious. Everett uses the form of the 19th-century adventure novel to say something urgent about freedom and identity.

One of the most discussed books we've ever had. Priya came with three pages of notes. It showed.

Group verdict: Instant classic
The Women
★★★

The Women

Kristin Hannah · reviewed by Marcus · January 2026

Historical Fiction

An ambitious novel following a female Vietnam War nurse across decades. The subject matter is genuinely underexplored, and Hannah's storytelling is propulsive. Pacing stumbles in the second act but the final third more than redeems it.

Some found it deeply moving, others felt it leaned too hard on sentiment. A genuinely good one for debate.

Group verdict: Worth the patience
